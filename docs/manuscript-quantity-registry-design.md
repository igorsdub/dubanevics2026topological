# Manuscript quantity registry design

The retained reconciliation baseline is job 53229644: the two Full graph
component quantities, Full and Published leaf counts, and four Published
full-library classification quantities were gaps because the pinned Full
landscape-summary product had zero matches. The retained table workflow records
those same eight keys, their baseline reasons and saved source identities in
`landscape-summary-reconciliation.json`. The Full library denominator is the
261,333 observed Full graph sequences. Table 1 reads the Full valley count from
`full_graph.persistence.valley_count`; the Published and Candidate valley cells
are gaps because no authenticated per-selection valley product is available.

Status: agreed 2026-10-23 in a grilling session; implemented, awaiting HPC run.

## Purpose

Every number the manuscript states comes from one generated source, so that prose, Table 1 and figure labels cannot diverge. Quarto later consumes the registry by key. Nothing in the manuscript is typed by hand when the pipeline can produce it.

## Artefact

A stage `manuscript-quantities` under the produce-or-load contract (ADR 0013) reads existing stage outputs and writes `output/tables/manuscript_quantities/manuscript_quantities.csv`, `manuscript_quantities.parquet`, `manuscript_quantities.schema.json` and `manuscript_quantities_report.md`.

The table is long: one row per reported quantity. Its columns are:

- `key`: dotted and fully qualified, for example `candidate_graph.peak_count`.
- `value`: the exact value.
- `display_decimals` and `display_value`: the value rounded, never truncated, to the stated decimals; counts stay unrounded.
- `unit`, `population` and `selection_rule`.
- `settings`: the keys of the setting rows the quantity depends on.
- `source_stage`, `source_fingerprint` and `git_commit`.

The report groups rows by topic and flags rows whose `display_value` changed since the previous run. It ends with a compact "manuscript numbers" list of `key = display_value` lines in manuscript order. A quantity the stage cannot source becomes a named gap row, never a typed value. There is no `values.json` for now.

## Quantities by topic

**Functional thresholds.** The published threshold is −0.507774 (Papkou et al. 2023). The candidate threshold T is the median plus the Median gap on the Full graph raw fitness, to six decimals, computed by the pipeline (ADR 2026-10-23). Rows cover the median, the global minimum g, the Median gap and the join-tree trailing saddle b, and the ordering g < b < median < T is shown.

**Graph sizes.** For the Full, Published and Candidate graphs: vertices, edges, components and largest component, each with its selection rule. The Full graph's components and largest component come from the saved Full landscape summary (Ticket 15 S4); until that product is present they are `missing:` gaps.

**Functional populations.** Counts and percentages under the rule "raw fitness at or above the threshold", with explicit denominators. Definitions match the graph summary table of PR #89, which becomes Table 1 regenerated from registry rows, plus components and peak count.

**Peak counts.** One per graph (`full_graph.peak_count` is the number of maxima of the Full split tree, read from the cached Full analysis). Historical schema 6 has nominal, core-rule and band-rule states; schema 7 has nominal and direct band states. The core-stage rows and `bootstrap.candidate.after_band_rule.absorbed_count` (the incremental core-to-band count) are historical schema-6 quantities only. Use `bootstrap.candidate.total_absorbed_count` for nominal minus after the band rule: every nominal peak that does not survive the threshold. The registry also records `bootstrap.candidate.after_band_rule.retained_sequence_count` (every sequence of the nominal tree, which the band view reassigns and never drops) and `bootstrap.candidate.after_band_rule.saddles_removed_count`. The identity-based selected-leaf absorption is not a manuscript number.

**Full graph persistence.** `full_graph.persistence.ordinary_h0_pair_count` and `full_graph.persistence.valley_count`, read from the component pairs. The ordinary H0 pairs are the descending sweep's peak-to-split-saddle pairs plus the global maximum's pair, closed at the global minimum by convention (Glossary: Persistence); the valleys are the relative pairs plus one for the global pair's mirror. The global pair is counted, by the repository convention that ordinary H0 persistence closes the global maximum's pair at the global minimum. They are separate rows from `full_graph.peak_count`, which counts split-tree maxima from another source, so the two can be compared but are never silently one number.

**Hills.** `published_graph` and `candidate_graph` `hill_sequence_count` (the sequences on all terminal branches, adjacent saddles excluded) and `largest_hill_sequence_count`, from the annotation function's `branch_vertex_count`; the singleton hills are `annotations.<graph>.singleton_peak_count`.

**Peak uncertainty.** The `peak_uncertainty` figure no longer reads the registry: each of its four panels prints the sequence counts, peak count, threshold and largest component stored in its own variant's `peak_panel_summary` product (ADR 2026-11-19-peak-analysis-per-variant), so the figure has no row in the Figure labels table. The registry still emits `peak_uncertainty.measured_sequence_count`: every row of the wild-type-relative fitness table plus the wild type, the one table both scatter panels draw.

**Functional threshold sensitivity scan.** The scan (`just functional-threshold-scan`, a tables-only export under `output/analysis/functional_threshold_scan/`, ADR 2026-10-24 Decisions 7 and 8) keeps its cells in its own tables; only its acceptance checks and the canonical interval are quoted, so the registry reads `acceptance.json` and `intervals.parquet` through `load_functional_threshold_scan` (never the cells) and adds `functional_threshold_scan.acceptance_a_passed`, `_b_passed`, `_c_passed` and `_all_passed` (booleans, shown as `true` or `false`; a failed check is a `false` row, never a gap), `canonical_threshold_interval_low`, `_high` and `_width` (fitness), `interval_width_limit` (2 x 1.96 x the median pooled standard error), `max_flip_fraction_one_step` (the worst of the five axes' largest flip fractions, among the axes that have a neighbour in the scan) and the three settings the checks cite, `resample_count`, `double_stop_below_minimum` and `flip_fraction_limit`. The check keys keep the ADR's single letters, the one exception to the no-single-letter-word rule of `KEY_PATTERN` (only `functional_threshold_scan.acceptance_a_passed` to `_c_passed`). Population and rule name the canonical cell (family and `k`) and the data of each check. When the scan has not run, or is not current for the settings and the classification checkpoint (the loader's refusal becomes a `stale:` gap), every row is a named gap row; the upstream's identity (settings, classification checkpoint, table hashes) is part of the registry fingerprint, so a rerun scan makes the registry stale. A canonical interval with no converged resample, or a scan with no neighbouring step on any axis, is an `undefined:` gap. The intervals table and check (b) of `acceptance.json` record the same interval; the loader raises when they differ.

**Bootstrapped persistence threshold.** Draws, seed, quantile, radius and value, under the rule "persistence strictly greater than the value", plus the quantile as an exact percentage (`bootstrap.band_quantile_percent`) and the range the scan figure shades (`bootstrap.band_quantile_scan.bootstrapped_persistence_threshold_min` and `_max`: twice the smallest and largest bottleneck distance of the nominal-landscape draws, by the stage's own `band_width`). The scan's threshold and surviving-peak count at the chosen quantile are `bootstrap.persistence_threshold.value` and `bootstrap.candidate.after_band_rule.peak_count`: the scan tables are checked against the checkpoint, so no scan-specific keys exist.

**Bootstrap global stability.** `bootstrap.global_stability.relative_wasserstein_1_median` and the sanity check (the flat-landscape draws, not the verdict): `...sanity_check.nominal_bottleneck_distance_median`, `...flat_bottleneck_distance_median`, `...flat_bottleneck_distance_fifth_percentile` and `...nominal_landscape_draws_below_flat_fifth_percentile_percent`. The registry stage raises when the summary's global verdict, band simplification and draw table disagree on the radius, the threshold, the quantile or the peak counts. The draw count the figure states is `bootstrap.draws`: the checkpoint reader refuses a draw table whose nominal-landscape rows number anything else.

**Summits and common saddle.** DNA sequence, amino-acid translation and raw fitness for the KEM summit, the TCL summit and their common saddle, displayed at two decimals. The common saddle is computed as the lowest common ancestor saddle in the Candidate split tree and asserted equal to the configured sequence.

**Ridge populations.** Per ridge and for their union: total, functional and nonfunctional sequences, with the saddle excluded. The two-functional-ridges share is the functional sequences in either ridge over the functional Candidate-selected sequences. The configured minimum share is reported as a boolean row; the code returns no verdict. The bootstrap codon-entropy figure builds its ridges on the band-simplified view, which absorbs more hills than the selected-leaf view of the codon-entropy stage, so its ridge counts are a different population: `ridges.band_view.kem.functional_count` and `ridges.band_view.tcl.functional_count` are computed by `analysis/band_view_ridges.py`, the figure's own steps (the in-memory Candidate tree built from the raw tables, the hills of the bootstrap checkpoint, the band-simplified view, then the codon-entropy population extraction); they are named gap rows when the raw tables or either upstream are absent.

**Annotations.** Counts of stop-bearing peaks and synonymous peaks (adjacent descending saddle, with persistence partner as a secondary row), each with its denominator, per graph and bootstrap state. Singleton counts are reported, not emphasised. The Published counts come from the annotation tables stage (`just biological-annotation-tables`, a prerequisite of `just manuscript-quantities`; no figure build writes the tables) and the Candidate counts from the same function run on the cached Candidate split tree; the core-rule and band-rule trees are not annotated (gap rows).

**Branches.** Terminal and internal branch counts and their sequence counts, from the branch decomposition. No Horton-Strahler order and no count of branches over 100 sequences.

**Leading saddle band.** The band is the closed interval between the split-tree trailing saddle b_split and the join-tree trailing saddle b_join. The split trunk is every sequence with raw fitness at or below b_split and the join trunk every sequence at or above b_join (both closed). `band_kind` is `overlap` when b_join <= b_split (equal included): the band is then the intersection of the two trunks. It is `gap` when b_join > b_split: the band lies in neither trunk apart from its closed endpoints. The band and trunk populations are therefore not a partition of the Full graph: in the overlap case they intersect. The band is closed and the intervals half-open, so `band_count` need not equal the sum of the interval counts between the two trailing saddles. The median's `median_sequence` is set only when exactly one vertex has that raw fitness, and is null otherwise. The stage `leading-saddle-band` (`analysis/leading_saddle_band.py`, `just leading-saddle-band`) computes them with one shared function, `tree_saddles`, which the noise-band figure's `TopologicalNoiseBand.lowest_split_saddle_death` and `highest_join_saddle_death` now call, so the registry adds no second computation. It writes `leading_saddle_band_landmarks`, `leading_saddle_band_intervals` and `leading_saddle_band_entropy` (each `.csv`, `.parquet` and `.schema.json`) and `summary.json` to `output/checkpoints/leading_saddle_band/` (exported to `output/tables/`, the summary as `leading_saddle_band_summary.json`). The summary keys the registry reads: `<landmark>_fitness` and `<landmark>_sequence` for the landmarks `global_minimum`, `split_trailing_saddle` (b_split), `join_trailing_saddle` (b_join), `median`, `split_leading_saddle`, `join_leading_saddle`, `published_functional_threshold` and `candidate_functional_threshold` (T); `band_kind`, `band_lower_landmark`, `band_lower`, `band_upper`, `median_inside_band`; `band_count`, `split_trunk_count`, `join_trunk_count` with `_percentage`; `interval_order`, `interval_counts`, `interval_percentages` (per consecutive-landmark interval, half-open except the last); and the positional entropy sums `band_entropy_sum`, `split_trunk_entropy_sum`, `join_trunk_entropy_sum` and `interval_entropy_sums`. Per-ridge entropy is not part of this stage: ridges belong to the Candidate graph. The real values are unknown until the stage runs on the cluster; this document states none. This replaces the "~180,000 sequences" claim. The figure `full_tree_fitness_landmarks` is a Full-graph raw-fitness histogram with the landmark ticks and the shaded band, drawn from the stage's tables; the interval table is `leading_saddle_band_intervals`.

## Implementation notes

Added with the stage (`analysis/manuscript_quantities.py`; the build command is `manuscript-quantities`, the recipe `just manuscript-quantities`).

- **Columns.** The twelve columns are, in order, `key`, `value`, `display_decimals`, `display_value`, `unit`, `population`, `selection_rule`, `settings`, `source_stage`, `source_fingerprint`, `git_commit` and `gap_reason`. `gap_reason` is the one column beyond the list above: null for a sourced row, the reason when `value` is null, starting with a kind (`stale:`, `missing:` or `undefined:`; see Gap kinds).
- **`value` is text.** It holds the exact value so the column has one type: integers as integer text, floats by `repr`, booleans as `true`/`false`, sequences as stored. `unit` (`count`, `percent`, `fitness`, `fraction`, `bits`, `boolean`, `dna_sequence`, `aa_sequence`, `quantile`, `seed`, `text`) says how to read it.
- **Rounding.** `display_value` is rounded with `decimal.ROUND_HALF_UP` from the exact decimal text of `value` (never through a binary float, never truncated; a tie rounds away from zero). The rules sit in one function, `display_spec`: counts and seeds are integers shown unrounded, a count with comma thousands separators ("262,144") and a seed verbatim; percentages 1 decimal; fitness 6; summit and common-saddle fitness 2; computed shares (`fraction`) and entropy sums (`bits`) 4; booleans, sequences, quantiles, text and configured values marked verbatim are shown as stored.
- **`settings`** lists, joined by `;`, the keys of the setting rows a quantity depends on (for example a functional percentage lists `thresholds.candidate_functional.value`).
- **`git_commit`** is the commit of the repository when the checkpoint was produced (`git_stamp`); a reused checkpoint keeps it. Config rows (the published threshold, the configured shares) have `source_stage` `config/analysis.toml` and no fingerprint; derived rows join the stages and fingerprints of their inputs with `+`.
- **Gaps.** Each gap reason starts with one of three kinds: `stale:` (an upstream output exists but is not current), `missing:` (the output is absent or no stage computes the quantity) or `undefined:` (the stage ran and the quantity has no value for its data); see Gap kinds below. An upstream that is wholly absent gives named gap rows; a present file that is malformed or incomplete raises. Standing gaps, which no stage fills: the Full graph's components and largest component (no stage computes them), and the annotation counts of the two bootstrap states (the annotation tables exist for the Published graph, and the Candidate graph's counts come from the cached Candidate split tree). The Full graph's peak count and the Candidate annotations are filled (`full_graph.peak_count` from the clamped comparison, `annotations.candidate.*` from its Candidate tree). The identity-based selected-leaf absorption is not a row. The band-view ridge counts are gaps only without their inputs.
- **Upstream freshness.** The clamped comparison, component analysis, peak uncertainty, bootstrap and branch decomposition are read through their cached loaders (`load_cached_*`), which refuse a stale or mid-publication output: "not found" becomes gap rows whose reason carries the loader's message (`<stage> output absent or stale: <message>; rerun just <recipe>`, since the loader also raises it for a stale checkpoint), any other failure raises. The leading-saddle-band stage is read the same way, through `build.load_cached_leading_saddle_band` (its checkpoint is `output/checkpoints/leading_saddle_band`, `summary.json` schema 1, fingerprint and artefact hashes in `provenance.json`); it is a declared upstream of the registry (the `manuscript-quantities` and `tables` recipes depend on `leading-saddle-band`). The codon-entropy and annotation tables are read raw. The registry fingerprint keys on the upstream's fingerprint and on the sha256 of every file read, so a changed file changes it even when a sidecar fingerprint is missing or reused. The band-view ridge counts are derived from the clamped comparison and the bootstrap (source `bootstrap-band-view`, fingerprint joining theirs) and computed only when read, so fingerprint checks stay cheap. The component pairs and the bootstrap draw table are read and their file hashes join the registry fingerprint.
- **Consistency checks.** The stage raises, naming both sources and the stage to rerun, when upstream outputs disagree: the configured published threshold against the clamped provenance `thresholds.study_clamped` and the graph summary; the Candidate `rounded_threshold` against the clamped provenance `thresholds.candidate_clamped`, the graph summary, the codon-entropy metadata and the band summary; the bootstrap's nominal peak count against the Candidate graph's peak count; and, when the band summary is present, its global minimum, median, T and b_join against the registry's `thresholds.*` values (exact equality). The bootstrap and the branch decomposition record only the Candidate threshold declaration, not its value, so no value of theirs is compared. Where the band summary is present it is the single source of g, m, h = m - g, T (rounded and unrounded) and b_join, and the same values recorded by the clamped comparison and the component analysis are compared with it exactly (they come from the same function, so any difference means a stale output). The bootstrap summary's blocks must agree (radius, threshold, quantile and peak counts of the band simplification against the global verdict, and the radius against the draw table's `band_width` at the band quantile); the split-tree maxima must equal the peak table's count for the Published and Candidate graphs; and the Candidate split tree must represent exactly the Candidate graph's vertices. Four population checks join them (see Population checks): the Full graph's vertex count against the peak uncertainty's measured sequences (scatter rows plus the wild type) and against the Full fitness table, the band view's core counts against the Candidate graph's vertex and functional counts, and the Candidate stop-codon peak count of the cached tree against the in-memory tree.
- **Key names.** `KEY_PATTERN` accepts dotted segments of underscore-separated words; each segment starts with a letter and each word is at least two characters (a letter then letters or digits) or all digits, so `aa_site_1` is legal and a single-letter word or segment (`x.value`, `thresholds.g_b.value`) is rejected. The ordering row is `thresholds.ordering_minimum_join_trailing_saddle_median_candidate.value` (formerly `ordering_g_b_median_t`).
- **Gap kinds.** Every `gap_reason` starts with a kind. `stale:` an upstream output exists under `output/` but is not current (its cached loader raised `FileNotFoundError` while the stage's checkpoint directory or provenance exists, or the annotation fingerprint cannot be computed); `missing:` the output is absent, or no stage computes the quantity (the Full graph components, the annotation counts of the bootstrap states, an absorbed count whose input the bootstrap summary leaves null, the band view when the raw fitness tables are absent). Only one `missing:` reason means the output awaits a run, and it has one canonical text, built by `stage_not_run_reason` and recognised by `is_stage_not_run` in `analysis/manuscript_quantities.py`: `missing: stage <name> not run` (a cached loader found no output at all, or the codon-entropy or annotation tables are absent, or the band view's clamped comparison or bootstrap is not run). `build figures` skips an opt-in figure as "awaiting HPC run" only for that reason; every other `missing:` gap (no stage computes it, raw tables absent) raises, as do `stale:` and `undefined:`; `undefined:` the stage ran and the quantity has no value for its data (an empty population, a join tree without an ordinary pair, an empty claim denominator). A row that depends on several stages takes `stale:` when any of them is stale. A stale or mid-publication output that a loader refuses with another error still raises. The report header states, in bold on its third line, how many gap rows are `stale:`, `missing:` and `undefined:`, and adds a bold WARNING line when any is stale; the numbers list omits gaps, so a stale count means numbers are absent, not old.
- **Annotation currency.** The annotation tables are rebuilt only by `just biological-annotation-tables` (a prerequisite of `just manuscript-quantities`, and read directly by the study biology and leaf figures, which take the study tables from `output/tables/` and the Candidate tables from `output/tables/candidate_annotations/`; so a fresh `output/` builds in order and a stale export is repaired before the registry reads it), so the registry checks them: `counts["landscape"]` must be `study_clamped`, and `peak_denominator` (and `peak_count` when recorded) must equal the clamped record's Published `peak_count` (exact, naming both sources and `just biological-annotation-tables`); the export record `.biological-annotation-provenance.json` must exist and its fingerprint must equal the current stage fingerprint, computed without running the stage by `build.current_biological_annotation_fingerprint` (the stage declaration keys on the comparison provenance digest and the annotation code). If the comparison provenance is missing the fingerprint cannot be computed and the annotation rows are `stale:` gaps.
- **Component cross-check.** The band's `join_trailing_saddle_fitness` is compared with the component analysis's `join_tree_trailing_saddle` whenever the component output is present, with or without the clamped comparison; the component's global minimum is compared with the clamped record's, exactly. When the component output is absent the band's value is kept and the `selection_rule` text of the two rows that use b (`thresholds.join_tree_trailing_saddle.value` and the ordering row) ends "; b was not cross-checked: the component analysis output is absent". `gap_reason` stays for gaps only.
- **Table 1 (retired).** The three-column graph summary that read the exported registry (`just graph-summary`, `tables/graph_summary.py`'s old exporter) was retired in Ticket 15 S4 for the seven-column landscape summary table, which reads saved products and not the registry. `require_current_registry` stays: the figures use it.
- **Resolved decisions.** (1) `>=` everywhere for the two-functional-ridges share: the codon-entropy claim table's column is `claim_meets_minimum` (formerly `claim_exceeds_threshold`, which used `>`) and agrees with `ridges.two_functional_share.meets_minimum` at exactly the minimum; the per-ridge position-2 enrichment rows have their own boolean `claim_exceeds_baseline` (strict `>` against the baseline; `claim_meets_minimum` is null for them and `claim_exceeds_baseline` is null for the share row), so each column name is true of every row where it is set. (2) The consistency checks keep exact equality, not a tolerance. (3) b in the ordering row is b_join, the join-tree trailing saddle; if real data put it above the median the row records `false` and the report shows it, with no special handling.
- **Report at export time.** `manuscript_quantities_report.md` is written when the checkpoint is exported, after reading the previously exported `manuscript_quantities.csv` from the destination folder. Because that previous run is not part of the fingerprint, the report is not in the checkpoint; a reused checkpoint that changes nothing leaves the report untouched, so its flags survive. The report is deleted when the export record is missing at entry (an interrupted export) and written atomically (temporary file, then replace), so a stale report never sits beside a newer registry. It lists a table per topic, flags rows whose `display_value` changed (and new keys), lists removed keys, and ends with the "Manuscript numbers" list of `key = display_value` lines in the order of the module constant `MANUSCRIPT_ORDER` (unlisted keys last; gaps omitted).
- **What `display_value` holds.** It is the complete printable number: rounding, thousands separators of a count (unit `count` only; seeds, sequences and other identifiers stay verbatim) and the sign live in it, and `value` stays the exact unformatted text. A unit symbol (`%`, "draws", "peaks") does not: the consumer appends it, naming the quantity from `unit` (Table 1 appends `%` to a `percent` cell, the figures write "{count} draws" in their templates). No current `display_value` carries a unit symbol. The schema description (`manuscript_quantities.schema.json`, key `display_value`) states the same rule.
- **Landscape summary reconciliation (Ticket 15 S4).** The registry reads the saved `landscape_summary` products of the two states the manuscript quotes, Full (nominal) and Published (point-estimate nominal), pinned in `[tables.landscape_summary.labels]` (source stage `landscape-summary-product`; rerun `just landscape-summary-product`). The Candidate graph is the `mixture_quantile` selection, not one of the seven states, and keeps its sources; the Median-reflected, BY and band columns are not quoted and have no registry rows (a band column would carry tree-level rows only, its graph-level metrics being those of the nominal column). New keys: `full_graph.component_count` and `full_graph.largest_component_vertex_count` (the Full landscape's diagnostics; the Full graph is one component holding every vertex, checked), `full_graph.leaf_count`, `published_graph.leaf_count`, and `published_graph.full_library_functional_count`, `_functional_percent`, `_nonfunctional_count`, `_nonfunctional_percent` (the Published point-estimate classification of every Full library sequence, percentage over the Full library). The existing selected-graph keys (`published_graph.functional_percent` and the rest) are unchanged. Equality checks (`_check_landscape_summary`): the leaf count of the saved tree equals the clamped split-tree maxima counted by `peak_count` (Full and Published); the leaves product's synonymous and eligible leaf counts equal `annotations.published.synonymous_peak_count` and `synonymous_peak_denominator` (one quantity computed by two code paths, so no second key); the graph node counts equal the clamped graph summary's. A difference raises with both numbers named and neither chosen: it is a finding to report and investigate, not to resolve by editing code. Limitation: a registry built where the products exist reads as stale on a clone without them (the products live in the Bucket archive, job 52520622; since PR3: 52655182), and there the new rows are `missing:` gaps, like the raw-table limitation below. By design a failed equality (a leaf-count or annotation-count check against the clamped and annotation sources, or a Full graph with more than one component) raises inside `check_consistency` and aborts the whole registry build, so no partial registry is written; the finding is to be investigated, not worked around. The percentages of the `full_library` rows take the Full graph (261,333 nodes) as denominator, not the 262,144-sequence library; whether the manuscript's "full library" means the Full graph is for the author to confirm.
- **Common saddle.** It is computed as the lowest common ancestor of the two configured summits in the cached Candidate split tree (`lowest_common_ancestor` in `analysis/codon_entropy_populations.py`, shared with the codon-entropy stage) and the stage raises if it differs from `reported_saddle_dna_sequence` or from the codon-entropy metadata.
- **Per-ridge positional entropy** comes from the codon-entropy stage's `papkou2023_codon_entropy_site_entropy` table: amino-acid entropy per position and their sum for the Candidate population and the KEM and TCL ridges (strictly above the common saddle, saddle excluded, nonfunctional sequences included), for all sequences and for the functional ones.

### Leading saddle band: interface read by the registry

The stage `leading-saddle-band` publishes its checkpoint to `output/checkpoints/leading_saddle_band/` with `summary.json` and `provenance.json`. The registry reads those two files; its source fingerprint is the checkpoint's `fingerprint` and the stage fingerprint of the registry keys on the fingerprint and the artefact hashes. While `summary.json` is absent every quantity below is a gap row with reason "leading-saddle-band stage output absent". The summary is flat:

- For each landmark `K` in `global_minimum`, `split_trailing_saddle`, `join_trailing_saddle`, `median`, `split_leading_saddle`, `join_leading_saddle`, `published_functional_threshold`, `candidate_functional_threshold`: `K_fitness` (row `leading_saddle_band.K.fitness`) and, for the five with an identity, `K_sequence` (row `leading_saddle_band.K.sequence`).
- Band: `band_lower_landmark`, `band_lower`, `band_upper`, `median_inside_band`, `band_count`, `band_percentage`, `band_entropy_sum`.
- Trunks (closed: at or below the split trailing saddle, at or above the join trailing saddle): `split_trunk_count`, `join_trunk_count`, `split_trunk_percentage`, `join_trunk_percentage`, `split_trunk_entropy_sum`, `join_trunk_entropy_sum`.
- Intervals: `interval_order` (names `<lower landmark>__<upper landmark>`, ascending by fitness) with `interval_counts`, `interval_percentages` and `interval_entropy_sums` keyed by name (rows `leading_saddle_band.interval.<name>.count|percentage|entropy_sum`), plus `below_first_landmark_count`, `above_last_landmark_count` and `interval_convention`. The names depend on the sorted landmarks, so an absent stage gives three placeholder gap rows `leading_saddle_band.intervals.count|percentage|entropy_sum`.
- Other: `total_sequence_count`, `median_gap`, `candidate_unrounded_threshold`, `candidate_threshold_rule`, `candidate_threshold_decimals`.

An entropy sum is the sum over the three codon positions of the amino-acid Shannon entropy in bits, and is null for an empty population (a gap row with its own reason). The registry does not compute b_split. Per-ridge entropy is not produced by that stage.

## Figure labels

Prerequisite 5, the figure half. Every number a figure prints comes from the registry's `display_value`, asked for by key through `figures/_quantities.py`; no figure formats a count or a fitness itself, and no label is typed in `config/figures.toml`. A label in these figures names the quantity in words (no Greek letters and no single-letter shorthand, so "bootstrapped persistence threshold" and "band quantile", never a symbol).

**Reading the registry.** `load_manuscript_quantities(output)` reads `output/tables/manuscript_quantities/manuscript_quantities.parquet` and never builds it; `from_rows(rows)` is the same view over in-memory rows (tests). `ManuscriptQuantities.display(key)` returns the row's `display_value` as text, and `requested_keys` lists every key a figure asked for, in order, failed requests included (the registry's own fingerprint, `registry_fingerprint(output)`, joins the figure's dependency record). Every refusal names the key and the registry path. The error classes are `QuantityRegistryMissing` (no file: run `just manuscript-quantities`), `QuantityKeyMissing` (not a row), `QuantityGapError` (a gap row, with its reason) and `QuantityStaleError`. Staleness is checked lazily and per requested key: the row's `source_stage` and `source_fingerprint` are compared with the fingerprint of that stage now (the same `load_sources` fingerprints the registry recorded, computed once on the first key that needs them), so a figure that asks for nothing pays nothing. A derived row joins its inputs with `+` and is checked against each; a row sourced only from `config/analysis.toml` has no fingerprint and is not checked per key. The whole-registry check covers it (a config change changes the registry's own stage fingerprint): `build_figures` runs it, and a reader outside the build, such as a notebook, loads through `load_current_manuscript_quantities(output)`, which runs `build.require_current_registry` and then loads (the plain `load_manuscript_quantities` skips it). `ManuscriptQuantities.value(key)` returns a row's exact `value` text for a figure that compares a drawn number with the registry; a sourced row with neither a display value nor a gap reason is malformed and raises `QuantityError`. The currency of the exported registry has one home, `analysis/manuscript_quantities.py` (`read_registry_fingerprint`, `require_current_registry`): Table 1 calls it with the current stage fingerprint and the helper reads the record through it, so the two cannot diverge. Gap reasons carry their kind (`stale:`, `missing:`, `undefined:`); the helper raises `QuantityStaleError` for a `stale:` gap row and `QuantityGapError` for the others. A figure that is part of the default build treats a gap or stale row as an error; an opt-in figure (`FigureSpec.default` false) skips with "awaiting HPC run" when the registry has not been rebuilt.

The table gives one row per printed label and key. A label that prints two numbers has two rows; figure identifiers are those of `figures/registry.py`. `tests/test_figure_label_registry_doc.py` keeps the table, the figures and the registry stage in step: it parses this table, runs each converted figure's own label test on synthetic inputs and checks, both ways, that the keys the figure asked for are the keys listed here; it checks that every listed key is one the registry stage emits; and it fails when a figure module formats a printed number with a format spec (`:,`, `.3f`, `.3g`, `.1%`, `g`) outside the exempt ticks and the layout diagnostic. A converted figure without a case in that test fails it.

### Population checks

Where the figure computes its own count, the population was read in the figure's code and the registry's definition, and one of the verdicts below applies. A new key means the populations differ or the quantity was not in the registry; an existing key means the same set.

- **Full graph peaks, sequences** (`tree_comparison`, `linked_tree_persistence`): new value for the existing key `full_graph.peak_count`, the `maximum` events of the cached Full split tree (the count every tree figure draws); the sequences are the tree's critical nodes and branch members, equal to `full_graph.vertex_count` (`tree_comparison` raises otherwise).
- **Published and Candidate peaks** (`tree_comparison`, `study_tree_persistence_*`, the leaf figures): equal to `published_graph.peak_count` and `candidate_graph.peak_count`. The figures count split-tree maxima, the registry the peak table of the graph; the stage compares the two counts for both graphs and raises when they differ, and the peak uncertainty analysis is cross-checked as well.
- **Published and Candidate sequences, functional counts** (`tree_comparison`, `study_tree_persistence_*`, `bootstrap_codon_entropy`'s core): equal to `<graph>_graph.vertex_count` and `functional_count`. The tree population is the selected component (`tree_population` raises unless it equals `selected_component_vertex_count`), the graph summary counts the same vertices, and both use raw fitness at or above the threshold.
- **Full graph persistence peaks and valleys** (`full_tree_persistence`): a distinct population. The figure counts the ordinary descending pairs plus the global pair as "peaks" and the relative pairs plus one as "valleys"; the label now says "ordinary H0 pairs" because "peak" keeps one meaning (a split-tree maximum). The global pair is included, as in the figure today (Glossary: Persistence).
- **Peak uncertainty measured sequences** (no figure prints it since `peak_uncertainty` moved to per-variant products; the registry keeps the key and its checks): a distinct key. The scatter table holds every measured sequence of the dataset plus the wild type for both panels, not the panel's graph; by construction (the same vertex builder as the Full graph) it equals `full_graph.vertex_count`, and the stage enforces that: `full_graph.vertex_count` must equal `peak_uncertainty.measured_sequence_count`, which is the peak uncertainty diagnostics' `fitness_wt_scatter_row_count` plus one (the scatter holds every measured sequence except the wild type, whose own row is excluded; the Full graph's vertices include it), or the build raises naming `just peak-analysis`. It must also equal the height of the Full fitness table (`load_full_network_fitness`, read through the `full_fitness_height` reader; the population `full_tree_persistence` sizes itself by), or the build raises naming `just comparison-analysis`. The Full-table check is skipped when the raw table is absent. The scatter's peak count equals the graph's, because every graph vertex has a measurement and the wild type is added back with its peak flag.
- **Stop-codon and synonymous peaks** (`study_tree_persistence_biology`): equal to `annotations.published.*`. The synonymous leaves the figure draws are the peaks whose amino-acid sequence equals their adjacent saddle's, the annotation table's `synonymous_endpoint_peak_count` (the figure's extra intersection with peak branches cannot remove one). The Candidate nominal tree's stop-codon count (`candidate_tree_bootstrap_biology`) is `annotations.candidate.stop_codon_peak_count`, from the same function on the same peaks.
- **Hills and singleton hills** (the leaf figures): a hill is a pair's `branch_vertex_count` (the leaf tables check it equals the hill's members), so singleton hills equal `annotations.<graph>.singleton_peak_count`, and sequences in hills and the largest hill are the new `hill_sequence_count` and `largest_hill_sequence_count`.
- **Bootstrap** (`candidate_tree_bootstrap_biology`, `bootstrap_global_stability`, the two scan figures): survivors are `peaks_after` and the nominal peaks `peaks_before` (the figure raises when the hill table disagrees); "absorbed" is nominal minus survivors, the new `total_absorbed_count`, not the core rule's or the band rule's step count. The retained sequences are every sequence of the nominal tree (`absorb_terminal_branches` raises when it loses or duplicates one), so the new key equals `candidate_graph.vertex_count`; it is kept distinct and checked against it. The scan's threshold and count at the chosen quantile equal `bootstrap.persistence_threshold.value` and the after-band-rule peak count (same `band_width`, same strict rule, tables checked against the checkpoint), so there are no scan-specific keys. The draws the global-stability figure counts equal `bootstrap.draws` (the checkpoint reader requires them).
- **Candidate stop-codon peaks of the bootstrap biology figure** (`candidate_tree_bootstrap_biology`): the figure counts them in the Candidate split tree it builds in memory from the raw tables (`build_candidate_tree`), the registry's `annotations.candidate.stop_codon_peak_count` in the cached Candidate tree of the clamped comparison. The band-view reader already builds the in-memory tree for the ridge counts, so it also counts that tree's stop-codon peaks (`in_memory_stop_codon_peak_count`, the figure's own `build_biological_annotations` call) and the stage requires the two counts to be equal, or raises naming `just comparison-analysis`. When the raw tables are absent the band-view rows are named gaps and the check is skipped.
- **Bootstrap codon-entropy ridges**: a distinct population. The figure builds KEM and TCL on the band-simplified view; `ridges.kem.*` come from the codon-entropy stage's selected-leaf view. The `ridges.band_view.*` rows count the band view's own populations. The figure's heading also prints `candidate_graph.vertex_count` and `candidate_graph.functional_count` for the core, and `band_view_ridge_counts` computes the core's total and functional sequences itself, so the stage requires them to be equal (to the clamped graph summary's Candidate `Number of nodes` and `Number of functional sequences`) or raises naming `just comparison-analysis`. The counts are read lazily (only with the in-memory tree), so the check is skipped, like the stop-codon one, when the raw tables are absent.
- **Scan figures** (`bootstrap_band_quantile_scan` and `_seeds`, the current run only): the marker position and the shaded range are drawn from `scan.parquet` while the printed text is the registry's, so the figure compares the scan's threshold and surviving peaks at the band quantile (and, for the single-run figure, the minimum and maximum bootstrapped persistence threshold) with the registry's exact `value` text through the registry's `agree` rule and raises on any difference.

### Exempt from the registry

These print a number but are not manuscript quantities.

- **Axis ticks and tick labels**: the top-axis quantile ticks of `bootstrap_band_quantile_scan`, the colour-bar count ticks (`_charts.py`) and every fitness or count axis. They are scale furniture.
- **Tooltips**: interaction text, not on the page.
- **Per-element data labels**: the branch count on every branch (`_count_labels.py`) and the per-peak labels of the leaf figures. They are the plotted data themselves; a count of branches comes from `branches.*` where the text states one.
- **Layout diagnostic**: the "dots overlap where a hill is wider than its column" line of `{published,candidate}_leaf_swarm` (`_overlap_note`), a property of the layout, not of the landscape.
- **`branch_decomposition_charts`**: notebook-only exploratory charts with no `FigureSpec`; they state per-order and per-branch counts.
- **`bootstrap_band_quantile_scan_seeds`** draws the current run only; the frozen 1,000-draw reference run (the Glossary's Frozen reference run) is no longer read or drawn, so no label formats a number of its own. The current run's numbers are registry rows (above), labelled "current run".
- **`reflected_fdr_trees` (`build_reflected_fdr_trees_figure`) and `bootstrap_four_graphs` (`panel_title`)**: the row and panel titles print a saved point value at the fixed three-decimal figure precision (and `reflected_fdr_trees` the nominal q in general format). That is a visible-label choice the user decides, and the registry displays six decimals, so these titles do not take registry display text.
- **`full_tree_persistence`'s noise band** draws the candidate threshold, the median and the global minimum as geometry (recomputed by `compute_candidate_functional_threshold`); it prints none of them.
- **The trail-label width** (`"10,000"` in `_charts.py`) is a layout measurement and is never drawn.

## Limitations

- **A registry built on the cluster reads as stale on a clone without raw data.** `load_full_fitness` returns no source when the raw fitness tables are absent, which changes the registry stage's fingerprint (the Full fitness table height is part of its identity), so a registry exported on the cluster, where `data/raw/` exists, has a different fingerprint from the one a clone without raw data computes. Take the registry from `data/processed/manuscript_quantities` (`just fetch-data`, then `just figures`) and do not rebuild it on a clone without raw data; a local `just manuscript-quantities` without raw data would also turn the band-view ridge counts into gaps.
